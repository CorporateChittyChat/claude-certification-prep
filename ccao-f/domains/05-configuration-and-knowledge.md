# Domain 5 — Configuration and Knowledge Management

*Companion video: EP 06 in the [series playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLFbwN1bk0S4c). The quiet domain — no clever prompts, just the setup that makes every prompt after it cheaper, faster, and safer.*

## The four moves

### Move 1 — Build the workspace

A project is a self-contained workspace: its **instructions** set the role and rules for every chat inside it, and its **knowledge sources** ground every answer. The quality of those two things decides the quality of everything the project produces.

- **Split projects by purpose** — sales materials in one, research in another, the weekly report in its own. Mixing contradictory materials in one workspace destabilizes every answer it gives.
- **Mount the recurring things permanently** — templates, past deliverables, standards. Explaining context every week is the tax this domain removes.

### Move 2 — Fence the knowledge

Uploading files is not enough. Without the fence, Claude quietly fills gaps from its general knowledge — and you can't tell which is which. So say it in the instructions: **"answer only from these materials, and say unknown for anything not in them."**

The same fence applies to connected sources: **narrow every connector to the minimum it needs.** Connecting a whole shared drive and telling Claude to "ignore the irrelevant files" does not work — the irrelevant files join the search anyway, and accuracy pays the bill.

### Move 3 — Write instructions a machine can hold

Three parts, kept **separate**: the **role** Claude plays, the **constraints** it must respect, the **shape of the output**. Keep them in separate tagged blocks — rules packed into one long paragraph lose their middle.

- When two rules can collide, **say which one wins** (conciseness beats completeness, or the other way around — but say it).
- The colleague test still applies: show the instructions to someone with no context; if they're confused, so is Claude.

### Move 4 — Maintain the setup

Configuration rots in silence: files go stale, sync sources drift, examples stop matching how the business talks. **Audit on a rhythm** — reread the instructions against what the project is actually for *now*, remove what expired, refresh what changed.

And be picky about memory: store the **decisions, preferences, and settled premises** — never the transcripts. A workspace that remembers everything remembers nothing well.

## Remember

- Instructions + knowledge live *with* the work; split by purpose.
- Fence: answer only from these sources; "unknown" is an answer.
- Whole-drive connectors feel thorough and quietly sink accuracy — the classic trap.
- Role / constraints / format in separate blocks; name the priority when rules collide.
- Setup rots quietly; audit, prune, refresh. Decisions, not transcripts.
