# Domain 6 — Governance, Risk, and Responsible Use

*Companion video: EP 07 in the [series playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLFbwN1bk0S4c). The stakes domain — the heaviest questions on the paper, and the ones your own company will quiz you on long after the exam.*

## The four moves

### Move 1 — Know the seven

The usage policy names **seven high-stakes areas** where output touches a person's rights, health, money, or opportunity:

1. Legal work
2. Healthcare
3. Insurance
4. Finance
5. Employment and housing
6. Academic testing
7. Media

In those areas, **two duties always apply**:

- A **qualified human reviews** before anything acts (the rule from Domain 2).
- **People are told AI is involved — at the start, not on request.** Anything customer-facing discloses, always.

The exam names a scenario and expects you to spot its area. Learn the seven like a phone number.

### Move 2 — Check the slant

On a sensitive topic, three tells give a biased output away:

1. Only **one side** of a live debate shows up.
2. Opposing views appear but get **dismissed in a line**.
3. A group of people is treated **unfairly or as a stereotype**.

The habit is one question, asked twice: *are both sides treated fairly?* and *would every group in this output recognize themselves?* Balance is checked, never assumed — and your review is the last line.

### Move 3 — Guard the data

Before anything enters any AI tool, **your organization's policy decides whether it may**:

- Personal information gets **anonymized**, or **explicit authorization**, or it **stays out**.
- Confidential business information passes the same gate.
- Regulated work carries one more duty: confirm outputs can be **logged and retained** the way the rules demand.
- Not sure? **Don't guess — escalate to compliance and ask.**

> On the exam: the answer that checks policy first beats every answer that starts with the clever part.

### Move 4 — Say the limits

When you present what you built, three things belong in the same breath: what it does well (**your own measured numbers**), what it **costs**, and where it still needs a **human, every single run**. Frame it as capacity moved to better work, never people replaced. If the value can't be quantified yet, pilot longer.

## Remember

- Seven areas by name; qualified review + up-front disclosure always.
- Disclosure happens at session start — "only if they ask" is the trap that sounds like balance.
- Three slant tells: one-sided, dismissed opposition, unfair group treatment.
- Policy decides what enters the tool; anonymize / authorize / keep out; escalate when unsure.
- Limits said out loud, with your own numbers.

*This teaches the exam's rules, not legal advice — your organization's own policy and counsel govern real decisions.*
