# The episode questions

One worked question per domain, from the [video series](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLFbwN1bk0S4c). Each episode opens with its question and closes by working it — try each one cold before reading the reveal.

> **These are original practice questions written for this series, in the exam's style. None are from the real exam.**

## Q1 — Domain 1: the format lottery

Your steering committee needs a forty-page vendor contract summarized. Every version Claude gives you is accurate, but the structure changes every time, and the committee wants one fixed format. What is the highest-value fix?

- **A.** Run the same prompt again.
- **B.** Add the line "do not change the structure."
- **C.** Give Claude three past summaries in the exact format the committee signs off.
- **D.** Move the whole job to a different tool.

<details><summary>Reveal</summary>

**C.** Formatting problems want examples — three to five, close to the real thing. A is the same lottery again. D is panic: the output is *accurate*, so the model is doing its job. **B is the trap** — it looks like an instruction, but it's negative phrasing about structure without ever *showing* the structure. (Domain 1, Move 4: fit the strategy to the task.)
</details>

## Q2 — Domain 2A: the statistic and the hour

Your client summary quotes a statistic from a named industry report. The deadline is an hour out. What is the highest-value next step?

- **A.** Ship it — the citation looks real.
- **B.** Ask Claude to double-check its own answer.
- **C.** Open the named report and confirm the number yourself.
- **D.** Strip every number out of the summary.

<details><summary>Reveal</summary>

**C.** One number, one source, a few minutes of your hour. A citation *existing* is not a citation *checked*. D guts a summary that might be perfectly right. **B is the trap because it feels diligent** — but confidence and accuracy are unrelated, and the checker is the thing being checked. (Domain 2A: verification means you open the source.)
</details>

## Q3 — Domain 2B: who signs the shortlist

Claude has ranked applicants for a role on your team, with clean reasoning beside every name. What happens before anyone acts on it?

- **A.** Use it — the reasoning is clean.
- **B.** Have Claude recheck its own ranking for bias.
- **C.** A qualified human reviews every recommendation before it touches a candidate.
- **D.** Use it only for the rejections, since those are quicker.

<details><summary>Reveal</summary>

**C.** Hiring is high-stakes work — a person who is accountable signs before anything acts. A: clean reasoning is presentation, not review. D is worse than it sounds: rejections are decisions about someone's livelihood, the exact place the rule applies hardest. **B is the trap** — rechecking itself is the same model marking its own homework. (Domain 2B: the human-review gate.)
</details>

## Q4 — Domain 3: the shallow analysis

You asked for a deep contract analysis. Every document was attached, and the answer came back shallow. What is the highest-value next step?

- **A.** Run the same request again.
- **B.** Jump straight to the most capable model.
- **C.** Give the same model more room to think, then climb a rung if it's still short.
- **D.** Switch features off to save cost while you retry.

<details><summary>Reveal</summary>

**C.** Effort first, escalation second — and a retry only when something new goes in. **A is the trap this time**: it feels like diligence, but nothing changed — same input, same model, same result. B works but skips the cheaper lever you were holding. D confuses cost management with unplugging tools. (Domain 3, Move 3: tune before you switch.)
</details>

## Q5 — Domain 4: the impressive rebuild

The weekly report process works, and a colleague proposes rebuilding it end-to-end around an autonomous agent. What do you recommend?

- **A.** Back the rebuild — the technology is ready.
- **B.** Change nothing — the process already works.
- **C.** Add Claude to the existing tools at the one step where it clearly pays, measure it, and grow it only if the results earn it.
- **D.** Keep the process but move every step to the most capable model.

<details><summary>Reveal</summary>

**C.** Simplest pattern, existing tools, measured growth. B: working isn't the same as improved — doing nothing wastes a real opening. D confuses model choice with workflow design (that was Domain 3's lesson). **A is the trap because it sounds like vision.** It's complexity before evidence. (Domain 4: augment the healthy.)
</details>

## Q6 — Domain 5: the weekly report setup

Same report, same document pile, every week. What is the right setup?

- **A.** Paste the documents into a fresh chat each week.
- **B.** Connect the entire shared drive so nothing is ever missing, and tell Claude to skip what's irrelevant.
- **C.** One project, instructions that restrict answers to its sources, and only the report's own folder connected.
- **D.** Upload everything once and never touch it again.

<details><summary>Reveal</summary>

**C.** Scoped, fenced, and maintained. A works exactly once a week, at full cost, forever. D freezes the world in place — and the world moves. **B is the trap because it feels thorough** — the irrelevant files join every search anyway, and accuracy quietly sinks. (Domain 5: fence the knowledge, narrow the scope.)
</details>

## Q7 — Domain 6: the launch question

A customer-facing support assistant built on Claude, and legal wants the compliance answer before launch. Which setup passes?

- **A.** Skip the AI disclosure — it would hurt customer trust.
- **B.** Disclose AI involvement only when a customer directly asks.
- **C.** Disclose at the start of every session, and route anything touching a customer's money or account to a human.
- **D.** Drop the assistant and route every chat to people.

<details><summary>Reveal</summary>

**C.** Told up front, human on the money. A is a straight policy violation, whatever the trust argument. D solves compliance by deleting the project. **B is the trap because it sounds like a compromise** — disclosure happens at the start of the session, not on request. (Domain 6: the seven areas and the disclosure rule.)
</details>
